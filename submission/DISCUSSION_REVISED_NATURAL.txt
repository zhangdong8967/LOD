Discussion

We scored fixed replicate counts against exact, author-specified operating characteristics. Exact Fisher power at 20 replicates per arm stayed below 20% in 61% of 1,225 equally spaced lattice pairs. As a 10-point example, 90%/80% had power about 0.07 and n* about 199 per arm. That 61% belongs to the design grid. It is not a laboratory failure rate, and it clusters at differences of 10 percentage points or less. Gaps larger than 20 points were rarely underpowered at n=20. The 20-435 range in Fig. 1C is a display scale for plotted n*. Cells with a 1-percentage-point gap sit outside it. A 19-of-20 result is a binomial pass event (pass probability 73.6% when p=0.95). It is not confirmation of a 95% detection benchmark under the study Clopper-Pearson rule. Reported replicate counts in a convenience audit of 16 publicly accessible rows were smaller than the Table 1 requirements. Those files had not stated an 80% power target. Replicate number has meaning only with the claim it is meant to support.

CLSI EP17-A2 specifies a minimal verification design and allows additional measurements when greater power is desired. The minimum number of observations needed to perform a procedure is not necessarily the number needed to detect a particular departure from the claim. Vaks et al. showed that pass probability for claimed-LoD verification depends on replicate number and on the relationship between claimed and actual LoD.[5] The present calculations extend that idea to two-arm hit-rate contrasts and keep pass rules separate from benchmark confirmation. The 19-of-20 criterion remains a qualitative acceptance rule, not a synonym for the EP17-A2 verification procedure, and does not revise the CLSI definition of LoD.

Quantitative reagent-lot studies make a related practical point. Algeciras-Schimnich et al., Katzman et al., and Kim et al. found that fixed protocols can miss lot differences and that EP26-type requirements vary by analyte.[6-8] Near the LoD the outcomes here are binary. Evidentiary value still follows the written claim and its operating characteristics rather than a fixed sample count.

Exact Fisher power was more than 0.05 below the pooled asymptotic calculation in 778 of 1,225 cells, so small-sample approximations need care near detection boundaries.[11] The retrospective audit supplies heterogeneous examples. The repeated claim-versus-n pattern supports using the lattice to quantify design consequences, without implying an incorrect clinical decision in any specific laboratory.

Resolve sample size when the claim is written. Lock the estradiol worksheet’s 19-of-20 sentence as a pass rule and n* is 21; lock it as Clopper-Pearson confirmation and n* is 53; lock it as a 90%/80% two-arm contrast and n* is 199. Protocols can separate a minimal EP17-A2 verification from a benchmark-confirmation objective or a two-arm comparison. Equivalence or noninferiority still needs an explicit margin.

A two-arm plan near 90%/80% that returns n*≈199 per arm need not be treated as a mandate. Laboratories can rewrite the claim as a pass rule or as benchmark confirmation, specify an equivalence margin instead of testing H₀: θ₂=0, restrict the comparison to a larger Δp that n=20 can support, or keep n=20 and report the exact power. The selected n has to be interpretable against the claim that will be signed.

Limitations

The audit is small (16 publicly accessible rows) and mixes replicate-level with aggregate data. Source files that omitted rates received assigned p₀=0.95 or the 90%/80% pair. No source stated 80% power, so Table 1 targets are author-specified. The 1,225-cell lattice is equally spaced and is not a prevalence of lot differences. We did not run a prospective, claim-locked verification on a laboratory specimen panel. Primary analyses assume independent Bernoulli outcomes with equal allocation. Logistic curves used to obtain p(c*) did not propagate fit uncertainty through the lattice. Binary hit-rate claims are in scope; blank-SD LoB/LoD, probit concentration estimation, and EP26 quantitative lot tests are not. The benchmark-confirmation rule is study-specific rather than a restatement of EP17-A2 decision criteria. Clustering entered only as a design-effect sensitivity analysis.

A nominal replicate count such as 20 only records how much data were collected. It does not give the probability that a prespecified claim will be detected, confirmed, or rejected. Across a broad detection-probability range we report exact operating characteristics for three binary hit-rate claim structures. EP17 continuous detection-capability procedures remain in place. The next practical test is routine hit-rate verification under a locked claim.
