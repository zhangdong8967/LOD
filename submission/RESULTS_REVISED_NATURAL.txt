Results

Exact operating characteristics across the detection-probability lattice

Enumeration covered all 1,225 unique two-arm probability pairs on the 50%-99% lattice (p_A>p_B) at 20 replicates per arm. Lattice cells are equally spaced design points. They are not an empirical distribution of laboratory lot differences, and the 61% underpowered fraction below is therefore a lattice property. Benchmark confirmation and qualitative pass criteria used that same conventional n. Operating characteristics for the three Table 1 claim structures are reported next.

Two-arm lot comparison at the limit of detection

At n=20 per arm, exact 2-sided Fisher β exceeded 0.80 in 61% of the 1,225 equally spaced cells (Fig. 1B; Table 2), so power stayed below 20%. The 61% fraction is a lattice property. Underpowering clustered where hit-rate gaps were small. Every one of the 235 cells with a 1-5 percentage-point difference, and every one of the 210 cells with a 6-10 point difference (including 90%/80%), had β>0.80 at n=20. Median asymptotic n* in those bands was about 2,900 and 425. Among 345 cells with an 11-20 point difference, 86% had β>0.80 (median n* 116). Among 435 cells with a difference >20 points, only 1.4% had β>0.80 (median n* 32). Thus n=20 can cover large detection-probability gaps. It is inadequate for the narrow contrasts that occupy most of this lattice, which is not a statement about how often those gaps occur in the field (Supplementary Table 3f). The lattice-wide median n* is also 116; that single number is not a recommended default, because n* is monotone in Δp. For the 90%/80% planning example (one 10-point cell, not the 6-10 pp band median), exact Fisher power was about 0.07 at 20 replicates per arm, and about 199 replicates per arm were needed for 80% power (Table 2). Fig. 1C shows the n*(p_A,p_B) surface on a 20-435 display scale; cells with a 1-percentage-point gap have much larger asymptotic n* and sit outside that range (Supplementary Table 3f).

Asymptotic pooled-proportion power exceeded exact Fisher power by more than 0.05 in 778 of 1,225 cells (Supplementary Table 3e). In selected near-LoD scenarios both arms could satisfy a 19-of-20 positivity criterion while exact Fisher power remained below the planning target (Supplementary Table 9c). Across 8 protocol-specific pairs, the median required sample size was 3.9-fold the 20-replicate comparator (Table 2; Fig. 2C).

Fixed pass criteria and observed positivity

When the true detection probability was 0.95, a 19-of-20 rule in a 20-replicate experiment had pass probability 0.736 (73.6%) (Supplementary Table 9), below the 80% planning target. Sample sizes that bring this rule to the operating target are in Supplementary Table 9b.

Benchmark confirmation of detection probability

Even 20 of 20 positives at a nominal detection probability of 0.95 left the lower one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson limit below 0.95. Under the study-specific confirmation rule, n=20 therefore failed (Supplementary Table 8). In all 12 benchmark-confirmation audit records, the exact probability of meeting that same rule at the archived sample size was 0 (Supplementary Table 7b). Few source documents stated this confirmation criterion. It is not an EP17-A2 decision rule.

Worked example from an archived protocol sentence

An Analyse-it EP17 estradiol workbook carries CLSI EP17-A2 Section 7.3 language: a minimum of 20 replicates at the LoD concentration, and at least 19 of 20 detected for a 95% detection claim. The audit mapped that sentence to qualitative pass criteria. At p=0.95, P(X≥19 | n=20)=0.736; holding k at 19, 21 replicates raise the pass probability to the 80% planning target. Scored instead as benchmark confirmation of p=0.95, 20 of 20 positives still leave the lower Clopper-Pearson 95% limit below 0.95, and 53 replicates are required for 80% probability of confirmation when the true rate is 0.95 (Supplementary Table 8). Read as a two-arm claim that a new lot matches a reference lot and scored at 90%/80%, exact Fisher power at 20 replicates per arm is 0.07 and n*≈199 (Table 2). Sample size is therefore undefined until the inferential claim is locked.

Retrospective audit of validation records

Sixteen document-level rows were audited: 10 tier A deposits plus institutional and published records (Table 3; Supplementary Table 7b). The set is a convenience sample of publicly accessible files, not a prevalence survey and not a prospective locked-claim experiment. In 15 of 16 rows the claim-specific requirement exceeded the conventional 20-replicate comparator (ratios 1.05-9.95). Claims mapped as 12 benchmark-confirmation, 3 two-arm, and 1 qualitative-pass. No source text named an 80% power target. Against the author-specified operating characteristics in Table 1 (α=0.05; 80% power), every archived sample size missed its corresponding target. That mismatch is a scoring result under Table 1, not evidence that those laboratories failed a target they had written.

Nine of 10 tier A deposits supported claim-specific calculation (required n 53-199; ratios 2.65-9.95). The 3 two-arm records (EP17 probit and 2 qPCR series) lacked written hit-rate pairs and were scored at the assigned 90%/80% planning pair. Exact Fisher power was 0.07 at archived n, with required n ≈199 per arm. One estradiol workbook mapped to a 19-of-20 qualitative rule stated in the source (pass probability 0.74). Remaining calculable deposits mapped to benchmark confirmation, usually with assigned p₀=0.95, and required more than 20 replicates; one linearity record lacked a detection-probability target. Six additional rows supplied replicate-level or published aggregate counts. Five of 6 empirical rates were below the 95% benchmark, and all 6 were evaluated under benchmark confirmation with assigned p₀=0.95. Empirical detection curves, required-to-reported ratios, and protocol-specific pairs are shown in Fig. 2.
